How it works

The loader cycle

One operator fills buckets in queue order. A loaded machine trams to the dump, empties its bucket and returns on its own. A bucket adds tonnes when it is dumped. A hang-up ties up the operator during clearing. If it needs a rockbreaker, the affected drawpoint closes and the loader waits or relocates.

Two exact checks

N is the loader count, F is mean fill time and A is mean autonomous trip time, both in seconds. With fixed times the fleet rate is limited by the loader cycles and the operator's filling rate.

Buckets per hour = 3,600 × min(N / (F + A), 1 / F)

With exponential times, the machine interference formula gives the probability P₀ that the operator is idle. Set r = F / A and sum over k from 0 to N.

P₀ = 1 / Σ [N! / (N − k)! × rᵏ]
Utilisation = 1 − P₀
Buckets per hour = 3,600 × utilisation / F

With enough drawpoints and no hang-ups, handover or other duties, the simulation matches the formula within 1 percent over 20,000 simulated hours for each fleet size.

Fragmentation uncertainty

Across 169 bench blasts, the median size estimate was off by about −30% on average. The middle half of errors extended from roughly −75% to +50%. Sanchidrián and Ouchterlony (2017)

Operating limits

Tonnes are a ceiling with no breakdowns. The model leaves out trucks, a full stockpile, breakdowns, ventilation and blasting re-entry.
